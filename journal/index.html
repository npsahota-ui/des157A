February 8th, 2026

The article "10 Intriguing Photographs to Teach Close Reading and Visual Thinking Skills" by Michael Gonchar dives into the importance of slowing down and actually taking the chance to fully understand a piece of media. These days, it's extremely common for people to want all the important information straight away, without having to do any of the thinking themselves. And as much as I hate to admit this, I can definitely be one of those people. However, accoding to the article, it's never to late to build on our critical thinking skills. Asking more questions, noticing details, and practicing regularly by looking at odd photographs can help us develop our skills. When it comes to design, using an intriguing image is also a great way to pull the user in.

A good example of this is the"M.V.P. Trans-Logistics" website. Visually it's magnificent and feels almost like a little adventure for the user. However, because it's so conceptual and different from your average website, navigating through the site feels unintuitive and may not be the most user-friendly site.

January 29th, 2026

After reading the article "Best Practice for Modals / Overlays / Dialog Windows" by Naema Baskenderi, I gained a new understanding of modal windows and the appropriate times to use them. The article states how these modals can be seen as annoying by users, and prompts them to immediately dismiss it. I couldn't help but agree, because I even find myself letting out a sigh when I come across a modal window. However,the article is extremely helpful with the tips it gives about when and how they should be implemented. The first and sixth tip were the ones that stood out to me the most, which were to have an "escape hatch" and make sure the modal window is user prompted. For me personally, these tips solve the problems that annoy me the most: having a hard time finding a tiny little "x" in the corner and having the modal shoved in my face randomly.

January 17th, 2026

Learning about the best practices when designing forms was extremely insightful and had me reflect on the many ways a small change in the design can make a big difference in the user's experience. The article "Best pratcices for form design" by Salim Ansari introduced a multitude of useful tips. One tip that I thought was interesting was how it's better to put placeholder text outside of a text box, because it helps users remember what the information they need to input should look like. Another interesting tip that stuck with me was minimizing the number of dropdown menus and to use checkboxes instead. This is because it minimizes the amount of interaction for the user and makes things easier on their end. One website that I think uses some of the best practices described in the article is Airbnb, as they add a simple "you won't be charged yet" underneath the reserve button to ease the user.
