February 28th, 2026

Afrer reading the article"I was asked to invent the next wordle. How hard could it be?"by David Shariatmadari, I learned about the many ups and downs he faced when creating the game Wordiply, which can be played when visiting The Guardian website. I appreciated that he shared his experience and what he learned during the process of creating this game, because I feel that it resonated with me as a designer. Reading about how he believed he came up with an original idea and was later told by someone that it's already been made was something I have been through countless of times. It was also interesting to read about the setbacks he faced with the development of Wordiply, such as how he had trouble trying to filter out the medical terms and words with over 25 letters that the average person wouldn't know. I have never taken it upon myself to look into game development, however after reading this article I have a newfound appreciation for the field. It is extremely difficult to find that perfect "sweet spot" between challenging and easy as well as keeping users entertained and wanting more.

To practice my visual thinking skils, I am going to examine a photo that my classmate took for our next class project.

This image showcases my classmates ear, which is filled with lots of fun piercings. According to her, this image conveys the impulsivity of youth and emphasizes the need to live in the moment. When I see this photo, I'm reminded that life is short and to live it up while I can. I think the most interesting aspects of the image is the contrast between human skin and the shiny metal piercings. I think it emphasizes how much the jewelry can enhcance someone's look and how it can be seen as a form of self expression. I also think the curves of the ear and the curves of the hooped earrings pair well together. The most obvious aspect of the images would be that it is just a large ear, and the most mysterious aspect for me is the color of hair in the image. It looks to be purple, further showcasing the need for self expression. I think some ways to make this even more visually enhancing is to play around with the lighting and see wha makes the pierchings pop, or even editing some more bold and crazy piercings onto the ear.

This is an image that I will using for my project, which focuses on the theme of nostalgia. I was doing some digging and found some old skecthes from when I was a little girl. I thought my little note about becoming an amazing artist was bittersweet, because these days I feel like I don't have time to draw every day like I used to. By only looking at this image, you wouldn't really know whether or not I achieved those dreams, or what kind of person I've become. I think I'm within the range of achieving them, being in design and all. And my art has definitely improved significantly since then, even though I produce it less often. My collection of photos tells a story about who I was as a little girl, and how I'm really not so different but from her at 21. To make these images more compelling, I think it would be fun to edit them to have a dreamy feeling, having things be slightly blurred and warm toned. Like recalling a distant memory.

February 8th, 2026

The article "10 Intriguing Photographs to Teach Close Reading and Visual Thinking Skills" by Michael Gonchar dives into the importance of slowing down and actually taking the chance to fully understand a piece of media. These days, it's extremely common for people to want all the important information straight away, without having to do any of the thinking themselves. And as much as I hate to admit this, I can definitely be one of those people. However, accoding to the article, it's never to late to build on our critical thinking skills. Asking more questions, noticing details, and practicing regularly by looking at odd photographs can help us develop our skills. When it comes to design, using an intriguing image is also a great way to pull the user in.

A good example of this is the"M.V.P. Trans-Logistics" website. Visually it's magnificent and feels almost like a little adventure for the user. However, because it's so conceptual and different from your average website, navigating through the site feels unintuitive and may not be the most user-friendly site.

January 29th, 2026

After reading the article "Best Practice for Modals / Overlays / Dialog Windows" by Naema Baskenderi, I gained a new understanding of modal windows and the appropriate times to use them. The article states how these modals can be seen as annoying by users, and prompts them to immediately dismiss it. I couldn't help but agree, because I even find myself letting out a sigh when I come across a modal window. However,the article is extremely helpful with the tips it gives about when and how they should be implemented. The first and sixth tip were the ones that stood out to me the most, which were to have an "escape hatch" and make sure the modal window is user prompted. For me personally, these tips solve the problems that annoy me the most: having a hard time finding a tiny little "x" in the corner and having the modal shoved in my face randomly.

January 17th, 2026

Learning about the best practices when designing forms was extremely insightful and had me reflect on the many ways a small change in the design can make a big difference in the user's experience. The article "Best pratcices for form design" by Salim Ansari introduced a multitude of useful tips. One tip that I thought was interesting was how it's better to put placeholder text outside of a text box, because it helps users remember what the information they need to input should look like. Another interesting tip that stuck with me was minimizing the number of dropdown menus and to use checkboxes instead. This is because it minimizes the amount of interaction for the user and makes things easier on their end. One website that I think uses some of the best practices described in the article is Airbnb, as they add a simple "you won't be charged yet" underneath the reserve button to ease the user.
